## 1. Summary

1. **No build tutorial or progress post exists in the search index for this exact costume.** I searched in English, Japanese and Chinese, and across YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, Reddit, DeviantArt, Amino and blogs. Every search returned only the MegaHouse figure pages, commercial costume listings and C.C. builds of *other* outfits. Use the figure photos and the commercial sets as visual references, and generic EVA-foam and fabric tutorials for the techniques (§2).
2. **Recommended build: a modular, low-sew costume** of removable units over a bought black maxi gown:
   - a stand collar with the throat gem
   - a "shoulder unit": an elastic figure-8 harness carrying the purple foam shoulder pieces and the white wings
   - a V-belt of foam plates on a strap
   - a separate streamer belt with 9 Velcro-on streamers
   - cuffs that strap over the detached sleeves

   Nothing rigid is glued to the dress itself. Each unit comes off for the U-Bahn, the cloakroom and the toilet.
3. **Effort:** about **45–55 hours hands-on** for a beginner [estimate]. That fits 12–31 Oct only if both weekends (17–18 and 24–25 Oct) are build days, plus 2–3 hours most weekday evenings. The fastest options (§5) bring it down to about **30–35 hours**. The streamers and their eye ornaments make the costume recognisable, so build them first. The cuffs and wings come last.

## 3. Build method, part by part

### 3.0 Tools (buy or borrow) [general technique]
- **Cutting:** a cutting mat; an 18 mm snap-off cutter with **lots** of spare blades (snap a segment every 1–2 m of cutting); sharp fabric scissors; paper scissors; a 50–100 cm metal ruler. A 45 mm rotary cutter is optional but makes long streamer strips much easier.
- **Shaping and gluing:** a heat gun; an iron and pressing cloth; sanding sponges (P120 and P240); clips (Wonder Clips or clothes-pegs); masking tape (Malerkrepp); cling film; a marker; tailor's chalk; safety pins.
- **Protective gear:** a **half-mask respirator with A-type organic-vapour filters (A1/A2)**, because FFP2 dust masks do not stop solvent vapours; nitrile gloves.

### 3.1 Base gown
- **Choice:**
  - A **black jersey maxi** (H&M €12.99 or Primark €16 **[snapshot, earlier notes]**). It is fitted, the cut edges don't fray, and it is cheap.
  - Horror-Shop **"Braut der Finsternis"** (black velvet, €27.95 S/M **[snapshot]**). It has a **white satin front panel**, so check where it sits:
    - On the skirt front, the front streamers can cover it, or it can be cut out to form the slit.
    - On the bodice, cover it with a glued-on black velvet or felt panel under the gold V-trim.
    - Never iron velvet face-down.
- **Front slit:** try the dress on in your party shoes and mark the slit to knee or lower-thigh height.
  - **Jersey:** just cut. It curls slightly but doesn't fray.
  - **Woven fabric or velvet:** fold the edges back and secure them with iron-on hem tape or textile glue.
  - **Both:** reinforce the top of the slit with a small patch of iron-on interfacing inside and 4–5 hand stitches across it, so it doesn't tear when you take stairs.
- **Hem:** it should clear the floor by about 1–2 cm in your shoes.
- **Gold V-neck trim:** two strips of 10–15 mm gold ribbon (about 2 × 30 cm) run from the shoulders to a point at the centre front.
  - **On velvet:** use textile glue.
  - **On jersey:** use 6–8 hand tacks per side, because glue on stretch fabric pops off.
  - **Either way:** pin the V first and check it in the mirror.

### 3.2 Patterning: cling film and tape (shoulders, belt, cuffs, wings, collar)
1. Put on the gown (and a bra) over the clothes you will wear underneath.
2. Wrap the shoulder area, hips and forearms in cling film. Cover it with **short, overlapping strips of masking or duct tape**, smoothly and without stretching [source: Skillshare / 405th].
3. With a marker, draw the **outline of each piece directly on the tape**: three stepped, angular layers per shoulder, the V-belt plates, and the cuff panels. Add a centre line and short registration ticks across every planned seam.
4. Cut the tape shell off along the outlines (a helper with blunt-tip scissors). Cut darts where it won't lie flat, flatten it and trace it onto paper.
5. **Make a paper or cereal-box mock-up and try it on.** Correct the size and the angles here, not in foam. Mirror each piece for the other side by flipping the pattern.
6. Mark grain arrows ("this edge = top") and the piece name on each pattern. Photograph them.

### 3.3 EVA foam parts: shoulder pieces, V-belt and cuffs
**Foam choice** [estimate]:
- **Shoulders and belt:** 40 × 40 cm EVA floor mats (Action, 6 for €6.97 **[snapshot]**; thickness not listed, similar mats are about 10 mm). Use the smooth side out and cut off the puzzle edges.
- **Cuffs and all trim:** 2 mm craft foam (Moosgummi).
- If the mats turn out to be 10 mm and feel too chunky for the lower shoulder layers, cut those layers from 5 mm EVA (if found) or from **two laminated 2 mm sheets**.

**Steps:**
1. **Trace** the patterns on the smooth side (mirror for left and right). Keep the long axis of each piece in one direction.
2. **Cut** with a fresh blade held vertical in one confident pass, with a few light passes for 10 mm foam.
   - **Bevels:** where you want the angular faceted look, cut the visible edge at about 45°. For two pieces meeting at an angle, bevel both mating edges so they close into a V-groove.
   - Smooth the edges with P120–P240 sanding or a Dremel on low speed, wearing a dust mask and goggles.
3. **Heat-form** (see the safety notes in §7.4):
   - Use the heat gun at its low stage (200–300 °C range [source]), held 10–15 cm away and **kept moving**, heating both faces for 20–40 s.
   - When the surface sheen changes slightly, bend the piece over a form: a bowl or ball for the shoulder cap, your own hip padded with a towel only after the foam has cooled to warm, or a large bottle or rolling pin for the cuffs.
   - Hold it until it cools (30–60 s).
   - Heat-forming also seals the surface pores.
4. **Glue the layers** with contact cement:
   - Brush a thin coat on **both** surfaces. Wait **10–15 min** (Pattex Ablüftzeit [source]), until the glue doesn't stick to a knuckle. Optionally add a second thin coat on EVA [source].
   - Align carefully, because the bond grabs instantly. Press hard.
   - Stack the layers like overlapping scales: top cap, then lower plates, each sticking out 2–4 cm further down the arm.
   - Avoid stressing the joints for **24 h** [estimate].
5. **Gold edging:**
   - Cut 2 mm foam into 6–8 mm strips with a ruler and fresh blade.
   - **Beginner trick:** seal and paint the strips gold **flat, before gluing** [estimate / general technique]. Touch-ups afterwards are quicker than painting a fine line on a finished piece.
   - Glue the strips along all visible edges with contact cement. Butt-join short strips; paint hides the joins.
6. **Seal:** heat-seal, then 2–3 thin coats of Mod Podge or PVA thinned about 50/50, or Flexbond (45–60 min per coat [source]). Plasti Dip spray is the most flexible option if you can get it [source; availability in Munich not checked].
7. **Paint:**
   - **Purple:** acrylic, 2–3 thin coats. Mix in a touch of black or white to match the reference.
   - **Gold:** metallic gold on the edges, 2–3 coats, because gold is translucent. A dark brown or black undercoat makes it look richer [general technique]. A gold paint marker (e.g., an Edding 750-type paint marker) is easiest for crisp edge lines [general technique].
   - **Finish:** optionally one coat of gloss varnish or gloss Mod Podge.
   - **Drying:** acrylic is touch-dry in about 20–30 min, recoatable after 1–2 h, and fully cured after about 24 h [estimate]. Don't stack painted parts before then.
   - **Spray paint** (faster, smoother): outdoors only, and Munich in late October is about 5–12 °C [estimate]. Below about 10 °C aerosols dry slowly and can blush, so spray at midday and bring the parts indoors to dry [general technique].
8. **Mounting:**
   - **Shoulder pieces:** glue a 10 cm strip of hook Velcro under each cap. It meets loop Velcro on the harness (§3.6). Add a 2–3 cm black elastic band from the lowest layer around the upper arm so the pieces don't flap up.
   - **V-belt:** make the front V plate and two hip plates, plus an optional small back plate. Fix them to a black webbing or strong elastic strap with a **foam sandwich**: glue a 2 mm foam backing strip over the strap on the back of each plate. Contact cement holds foam to foam; it is unreliable on bare webbing. Close the strap at the back with 10 cm of Velcro, or with a side-release buckle hidden under the streamers.
     - **Leave 1–2 cm gaps between plates** so you can sit.
     - Keep the V-point **at least 5 cm above the hip crease**, or it digs in when you sit.
   - **Cuffs:** 2 mm foam, two layers, heat-curved to the forearm, closed with Velcro around the arm **over** the sleeve. Do not glue them to the sleeve: stretch fabric pops glue. Place them just below the elbow so the elbow still bends.

### 3.4 Streamers (9 × about 12 cm wide, pointed tips)
**Length:** you gave about 1.3 m. Measure **waist-to-floor in your shoes and subtract 3–5 cm**. For a 165–170 cm wearer that is typically about 1.05–1.20 m [estimate]. Make the **front streamers 5–10 cm shorter** than the back ones (§7.1).

**Option A, best look (low-sew):**
- **Fabric:** a white medium-weight woven, such as cotton twill or drill, poly-cotton canvas or gabardine (140–150 cm wide).
- **Stiffening:** **Vlieseline H250** on the back. It is a stable fusible for collars and belts, 90 cm wide, about €5.85–6.90/m [source: VBS, etissus].
- **Edges:** gold bias tape. Lurex 20 mm on 25 m rolls is about €1.04/m [source: eBay snapshot].

1. Cut the interfacing strips first (12 × 140 cm). Fuse them to the fabric with the iron on a wool setting and a damp pressing cloth, holding about 8–10 s per spot without sliding [general technique]. Let it cool flat.
2. Cut out each streamer along the interfacing edge with a rotary cutter and ruler.
3. **Point:** mark 6 cm up from the bottom on each long edge and cut to the centre of the bottom edge (about a 90° point).
4. **Gold edge:** fold the bias tape over the raw edge and secure it with either:
   - **iron-on hem tape (Bügelband)** inside the fold, or
   - a thin bead of textile glue (Gütermann HT2 or Prym; let it cure about 24 h [earlier notes]), clipped every 10 cm while it dries.

   Mitre the tape at the tip (fold it into a neat V). The tape hides raw edges on both faces, which matters because streamers flip when you walk.
5. **Top:** leave 10 cm unbound. Fold it back into a 5 cm tab and glue **5–6 cm of loop Velcro** on the inside face. Add 4 hand stitches through the Velcro: adhesive Velcro alone peels off fabric.

**Option B, fastest:**
- **Fabric:** **white PU faux leather** (what commercial sets use [source]) or 1–2 mm white felt. Neither frays and neither needs interfacing.
- **Cutting:** cut with a rotary cutter, then add the point and the Velcro tab as above.
- **Gold edge:** **paint** it instead of taping:
  - Mask a 1 cm border with masking tape on each side.
  - Sponge on gold textile or leather paint (or acrylic with textile medium), 2 coats.
  - Peel the tape while the paint is just touch-dry.
- **Felt:** pills and looks fuzzy up close. Faux leather looks closest to the reference.

**Streamer belt:**
- **Strap:** 2.5–4 cm black webbing or strong elastic at waist length + 15 cm, with a Velcro or buckle closure. Glue **hook Velcro tabs** on the outside at even spacing: about every 8–9 cm for a 75 cm waist [estimate].
- **Layout:** 2 at the front either side of the slit, 2 on each hip, 3 at the back.
- **Wearing it:** the streamer belt goes on first. The V-belt goes over it and hides the tabs. The Velcro also works as a **breakaway**: if someone steps on a streamer, it pulls off instead of tearing.
- **Snaps instead of Velcro:** cleaner, but they need setting pliers (Prym or KAM snaps) [general technique].

### 3.5 Eye ornaments (9 + 2 spares)
- **Size:** an almond or diamond "eye" about 9 × 5.5 cm outer for a 12 cm streamer [estimate], with a 7–8 mm gold frame.
1. Make **one cardboard template** for the outer eye and one for the inner opening. Trace 11 of each on 2 mm foam.
2. **Base:** the full eye shape in 2 mm foam, painted black. Black foam saves a step.
3. **Frame:** the eye outline with the inner opening cut out, sealed and painted gold, or cut from **gold metallic foam** so it needs no paint.
4. **Iris:** a red circle, Ø 2–2.5 cm: red foam, or a **red flat-back rhinestone** (14–18 mm) for the "clear parts" look of the figure [source: Otakumode]. Add a black pupil (dot or slit) with a paint marker.
5. Glue frame on base, then iris on top, with contact cement or superglue gel. Finish with a **gloss coat** (gloss Mod Podge or clear nail varnish) for an enamel look.
6. **Attach** each ornament 10–15 cm above its streamer's tip with contact cement plus 2 hand stitches, or with Velcro dots if you want them removable. A little weight near the tip also helps the streamer hang straight.

### 3.6 Wing collar and shoulder harness
**Size:** make a paper mock-up first. A typical size is about 35–40 cm tall above the shoulder line × 22–28 cm wide per wing, flaring outward [estimate; scale from the reference photo].

**Core (pick one):**
1. **EVA:** one 5 mm sheet, or two 2 mm sheets laminated with contact cement. Heat-curve it to flare outward. It is light, and it bends back when bumped. It needs a sheet of at least 40 × 50 cm, larger than the A4 or 40 × 40 sizes.
2. **PP plastic:** polypropylene file folders or a PP sheet (≥ A3, or two A4 pieces joined with clear tape). This is the lightest and springiest option. Contact cement does not bond PP [source: Pattex TDS], so cover it with fabric using textile glue or double-sided tape.
3. **No-foam:** two layers of stiff fusible interfacing (**Vlieseline S520**, 45 or 90 cm wide [source]; or **Decovil I**, about €12.72/m by the roll [source]). Fuse them between white fabric, and run 1.5–2 mm craft or garden wire along the edge under the binding for shape. This option packs flat.

**Cover:** white fabric on both faces, matching the streamers. Glue the front piece with 1.5 cm extra wrapped to the back, then glue the back piece trimmed exactly to size. Edge it with gold bias tape or 2 mm gold foam strip.

**Mounting:** use a **figure-8 elastic harness** (low-sew):
- About 1.6 m of 3 cm black elastic forms two loops around the shoulders that cross between the shoulder blades. Join it with 6 hand stitches per joint or two strong safety pins.
- At the crossing, glue or pin a 15 × 10 cm **back plate** (5–10 mm foam with a felt backing).
- Each wing has a 6–8 cm tab at its base that Velcros onto the back plate. A short strap or elastic between the two wings, at shoulder-blade height, stops them flopping outward.
- The shoulder pieces Velcro onto loop Velcro glued or stitched to the top of each harness loop.
- **Simplest alternative:** glue the wing tabs to the inside back edge of each shoulder cap, and safety-pin each cap to the dress's shoulder seam through a felt tab. It is quicker, but the weight drags a jersey dress out of shape [estimate].
- **Wig:** the wig hair hangs between the wings. Keep **hook** Velcro covered or facing away from the wig, because it shreds wig fibre and stockings.

### 3.7 Stand collar, gold edging and the throat gem
- **Core:**
  - Two laminated 2 mm EVA strips, **or** S520 or Decovil fused between black fabric.
  - About 8 cm tall at the front and 9–10 cm at the back, and as long as your neck circumference + 3 cm overlap.
  - Cut it as a **gentle arc** (top edge 2–4 cm longer than the bottom) so it flares away from the chin [general technique].
  - Paint EVA black after sealing, or cover it in black fabric. Line the inside with soft black felt or jersey.
- **Comfort:** leave 1–2 cm of ease, so two fingers fit. Check that you can turn your head and swallow.
- **Edging:** gold 2 mm foam strip or gold ribbon along the top and bottom. Close at the back with 5 cm of Velcro, hidden under the wig.
- **Gem:**
  - A red oval acrylic cabochon or flat-back rhinestone (about 18 × 25 to 30 × 40 mm) [estimate]. As a DIY version, use a 2 mm foam oval painted red with a heavy gloss coat and a white highlight dot.
  - Set it in a gold 2 mm foam bezel ring and glue it at the front centre, low on the collar, with superglue gel or E6000-type glue. E6000 needs 24–72 h to cure [estimate], so do this by **Sun 25 Oct**.

### 3.8 Detached sleeves
- **Easiest:** **black hold-up (thigh-high) stockings worn on the arms**. The silicone band grips the upper arm, so there is nothing to sew [general technique].
  - Cut a thumb hole: a 1.5 cm snip at the right point. Tights and jersey don't fray, though fine stockings can ladder from the cut.
  - Put a drop of clear nail varnish on the hole edge.
- **Alternatives:**
  - Opaque 60–100 den tights legs, cut off and held up with a black elastic band or garter.
  - Arm warmers.
  - The sleeves of a thrifted black long-sleeved top.
  - Black satin opera gloves, if you want a glossy look.
- The foam cuffs (§3.3) strap on over them.

### 3.9 Stockings, shoes and wig
- **Stockings:** **white thigh-high stockings with a silicone band.** Buy **2 pairs**, since white shows ladders and dirt. White overknee 2-packs are about €10–13 online **[snapshot, earlier notes]**. For warmth in late October, white opaque tights under the slit look nearly the same [estimate].
- **Shoes:** low block heels or comfortable boots. White boot covers (Stiefelstulpen, about €14–17 **[snapshot, earlier notes]**) can turn ordinary shoes into "white boots".
- **Wig:** a long lime-green wig. Options:
  - EZcosplay 80 cm, €18.45
  - Buttinette 65 cm, €24.95, the safest delivery
  - Amazon.de XINYIYI 100 cm, €30

  All are **[snapshot, earlier notes]**. Order **by Tue 13 Oct**. Trim the fringe only after a full try-on with the collar and wings.

## 4. Material quantities (with assumptions)

### Assumptions [estimate]
- Wearer about 165–170 cm, waist about 70–80 cm.
- 9 streamers, finished **1.30 m** (the user's figure, which is generous) + **10 cm** for the Velcro tab, so a **cut length of 1.40 m**. Width 12 cm. The tip is a 6 cm-tall point.
- Edging perimeter per streamer (excluding the top) = 2 × 1.24 m + 2 × 0.085 m (tip) ≈ **2.65 m**. For 9 streamers that is **≈ 24 m**.
- 40 × 40 cm floor mat: usable ≈ 36 × 36 cm ≈ **1,300 cm²** after the puzzle edges are removed. A 30% nesting and waste allowance is applied to all foam.

## 7. Practical tips

### 7.1 Wearability at a party
- **Walking:**
  - The front slit is essential. The front streamers end **5–10 cm above the floor in your shoes**, and the back ones can reach the floor.
  - Walk with a slightly longer stride, and kick the front streamers forward on stairs. On **stairs and escalators**, gather the skirt and front streamers in one hand. Long fabric near the escalator comb plate at the top or bottom is a real snag hazard.
- **Sitting:**
  - Flip the back streamers to the sides or up over the chair back before sitting, or pull the 3 back streamers off (Velcro).
  - Use **medium** interfacing (H250). Very stiff streamers crease permanently when sat on.
  - The V-belt gaps and the high V-point stop it digging in.
  - Wings: sit forward or on a stool, since a chair back pushes them up.
- **Toilet:**
  - Practise once at home.
  - Take off the streamer belt and V-belt as one bundle and hang it on the cubicle hook (an S-hook or carabiner helps). Detach the wings if the cubicle is narrow, then lift the gown.
  - Wear the stockings with a silicone band rather than a suspender belt, which is harder to manage in a cubicle.
- **Crowds:**
  - Total width with wings should stay within about your shoulder width + 10 cm per side [estimate].
  - Wings made of EVA or PP bend back instead of snapping, and Velcro lets them detach rather than break.
  - Streamers on Velcro pull off rather than tear when stepped on, so count them when you leave.
  - Keep sharp foam points away from eye level.
- **Cloakroom:** the shoulder-and-wing unit is awkward to check in. Plan to wear it and check only your coat and transport bag. Some cloakrooms charge per item.
- **Warmth (late October):** wear a long coat over the gown for the trip. Put the wings and shoulders on at the venue. Add white opaque tights or thermal leggings under the gown.

### 7.2 Transport on the U-Bahn
- Travel in the gown, sleeves, stockings, collar and wig under a long coat. Carry the **harness + shoulders + wings** in a large IKEA bag or a flat art portfolio (Zeichenmappe A2), and the **streamer belt** rolled loosely (don't fold the interfaced strips sharply) or hung in a garment bag.
- Alternatively, dress fully at a friend's place near the venue.
- Halloween Saturday-night trains are crowded. Stand clear of the doors, and keep streamers and wig hair out of the door gap.
- Munich escalator etiquette is to stand on the right. Hold the gown and streamers on escalators (see above).
- MVG night-service times were **not checked**. Look up your last train or night bus beforehand.

### 7.3 Repair kit (fits in a small pouch)
- **Glue and fasteners:**
  - superglue gel (foam and gems)
  - a small textile glue tube
  - double-sided fashion or carpet tape
  - 20 assorted safety pins
  - spare adhesive Velcro dots and a 20 cm strip of sew-on Velcro
- **Sewing:** needle with black and white thread pre-threaded, mini scissors.
- **Touch-ups:**
  - gold paint marker, black and purple markers (scuffs)
  - stain-remover pen and wet wipes (white streamers)
  - a lint roller
- **Spares:**
  - clear nail varnish (stocking ladders)
  - **a spare pair of white stockings**
  - spare elastic (30 cm)
- **Wig:** bobby pins, wig clips, a small brush or comb.
- **Personal:** blister plasters, a power bank, a small mirror, and a fold-up bag for detached parts.

### 7.4 Safety
- **Contact cement and sprays (solvent-based):**
  - Work outdoors or on a balcony, or with windows open for a cross-draught. Use small batches and close the tin between uses.
  - **No open flames**, which includes Halloween candles and gas hobs [source: forum and DIY guides; label].
  - Wear an **A-filter respirator** and nitrile gloves. FFP2 masks don't stop vapours.
  - Keep children and pets away. Let rags dry outdoors before binning them.
- **Heat gun:**
  - The nozzle is hot enough to burn skin and stays hot for minutes after switch-off. Rest it on its stand on a heat-proof surface (tile or metal tray).
  - Never point it at people, hair or **the wig**, because synthetic wig fibre melts.
  - Keep it moving and use the low stage. Stop if the foam smokes or blisters badly (> 300 °C burns EVA [source]).
  - Don't heat foam while it is on your body. Form it over a form, or let it cool to warm before shaping it on yourself.
- **Floor-mat foam:** unpack the Action mats a few days early and air them. Cheap foam mats can off-gas formamide, mostly in the first days, and heat raises the level [source: ANSES, HK Consumer Council]. Heat-form them in a ventilated room.
- **Cutting and sanding:**
  - Use fresh blades and always cut away from your hand, on a mat. Snap old blades into a jar.
  - Wear goggles and a dust mask for Dremel or sanding work.
